# Code of Conduct

This Code of Conduct defines the behavioral standards for everyone who
participates in the
[`quanticsoul4772/hygiene-streak-target`](https://github.com/quanticsoul4772/hygiene-streak-target)
community — in issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code
review comments, and any other space associated with this repository.

## 1. Our pledge

We as members, contributors, and maintainers pledge to make participation in
this community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age,
body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics,
gender identity and expression, level of experience, education,
socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or
sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open,
welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## 2. Expected behavior

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment include:

- **Being kind and respectful** — demonstrating empathy toward other people
  and treating disagreement as a difference of view, not a personal attack.
- **Giving and accepting constructive feedback gracefully** — critiquing
  ideas and code, not people, and receiving critique in the same spirit.
- **Being welcoming to newcomers** — answering beginner questions patiently
  and pointing people to relevant documentation rather than dismissing them.
- **Taking responsibility** — apologizing to those affected by our
  mistakes and learning from the experience.
- **Focusing on what is best for the community** — not just for ourselves
  as individuals.
- **Respecting others' time and boundaries** — keeping discussions
  on-topic and accepting "no" as an answer.

## 3. Unacceptable behavior

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

- Harassment, intimidation, or discrimination in any form, public or
  private.
- The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or
  advances of any kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political
  attacks.
- Publishing others' private information — such as a physical address or an
  email address — without their explicit permission ("doxxing").
- Deliberate misgendering or use of rejected names.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, reviews, or other community
  activities, including spamming issues or pull requests.
- Advocating for, or encouraging, any of the above behavior.
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## 4. Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all repository spaces (issues, pull
requests, discussions, code review, and commit history), and also applies
when an individual is officially representing the community in public
spaces — for example, using an official project account or acting as an
appointed representative at an event.

## 5. Reporting a violation

Conduct reports are handled through the **same private channel this
repository already uses for security reports** (see
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md)) — there is no separate conduct contact, and
this keeps sensitive reports out of public view:

1. Open the repository's
   [Security advisories page](https://github.com/quanticsoul4772/hygiene-streak-target/security/advisories).
2. Click **Report a vulnerability**, note clearly at the top of the form
   that this is a **Code of Conduct report** (not a security
   vulnerability), and describe the incident.

This sends the report privately to the maintainer, and GitHub provides a
private channel for any follow-up discussion.

If you are unable to use private reporting, you may instead open a plain
issue that says only that you have a conduct concern and asks the
maintainer to get in touch — **do not include incident details, names, or
accusations in the public issue itself**.

**Please do not report conduct incidents through public GitHub issues,
discussions, or pull requests.** Public reports can expose reporters and
others involved to further harm before the maintainer can act.

## 6. What to include in a report

There is no report template, so plain free-form reports are welcome. To
help the maintainer understand and act on your report quickly, please
include:

- a note that this is a **Code of Conduct report**;
- your contact preference for follow-up (the private thread is the
  default);
- a description of the incident: what happened, and why you believe it
  violates this Code of Conduct;
- where and when it happened, with links to the relevant issues, pull
  requests, comments, or other records where possible;
- who was involved (usernames), and whether the behavior is ongoing;
- any supporting context such as screenshots of since-deleted content; and
- whether you would like your identity kept confidential from the person
  reported (it will be, by default, to the extent GitHub's tooling allows).

## 7. What to expect after reporting

This repository is maintained by a single maintainer on a best-effort
basis, so response times are pragmatic rather than guaranteed — the same
handling commitments made in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) apply:

- **Acknowledgment** — you should receive an acknowledgment of your report
  within **7 days**.
- **Review** — the maintainer will review the report, examine the linked
  material, and, where appropriate, hear from the person reported before
  reaching a conclusion.
- **Resolution** — the maintainer will aim to resolve confirmed violations
  within **30 days**, keeping you informed of progress through the private
  report thread, and will tell you the outcome.

All reports are handled with discretion. The maintainer will keep the
reporter's identity confidential by default and will act in good faith to
protect reporters from retaliation — retaliation against anyone who reports
a concern is itself a violation of this Code of Conduct.

If you have not heard back within 7 days, please follow up on the private
report thread — a reminder is welcome, not a nuisance.

## 8. Enforcement and consequences

The maintainer is responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards
and will take fair, proportionate corrective action in response to any
confirmed violation. The maintainer may remove, edit, or reject comments,
commits, code, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned with
this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions
when appropriate.

Consequences for confirmed violations escalate as follows:

### Level 1 — Correction

**Applies to:** use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed
unprofessional or unwelcome, typically a first-time, lower-impact incident.

**Consequence:** a private, written warning from the maintainer, providing
clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the
behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested. Offending
content may be edited or removed.

### Level 2 — Warning

**Applies to:** a violation through a single significant incident or a
series of repeated lower-level incidents.

**Consequence:** a formal warning with stated consequences for continued
behavior. No interaction with the people involved — including unsolicited
interaction with those enforcing this Code of Conduct — for a specified
period of time. Violating these terms leads to a temporary ban.

### Level 3 — Temporary ban

**Applies to:** a serious violation of community standards, including
sustained inappropriate behavior or ignoring a prior warning.

**Consequence:** a temporary ban (via GitHub's blocking and interaction
limit tools) from any sort of interaction or public communication with the
repository for a specified period of time. Violating these terms leads to a
permanent ban.

### Level 4 — Permanent ban

**Applies to:** demonstrating a pattern of violation of community
standards, sustained harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or
disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence:** a permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within
the repository and its community spaces.

The maintainer may enter the ladder at any level appropriate to the
severity of the confirmed violation — a sufficiently serious first offense
(for example, doxxing or threats) may result in an immediate temporary or
permanent ban.
